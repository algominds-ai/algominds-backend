# The four blocks, in order

Write the description as four paragraphs. Nothing else goes in it.

## 1. Who the buyer is, and the buyer test

Name the kinds of organisation. Then one sentence that separates a customer
from a competitor by asking **who owns the end customer relationship**, never
by asking whether the company touches the seller's category.

> Companies that hold the end customer relationship and move that customer's
> money themselves... A company that sells payment processing to other
> financial institutions is a competitor and never qualifies. If a company's
> customers are themselves banks or payment providers, exclude it. If its
> customers are consumers or merchants, include it.

## 2. The shape that qualifies

Sizes, geographies, and any segment the seller says is cold. Sellers often
have two disjoint segments with a dead zone between them; say so when they do.

> A growing agency of roughly 5 to 50 people, where saving a seat is a real
> decision. Or an employer of 100 or more, which has budget and wants coverage.
> Treat a firm with 200 or more in-house recruiters as cold.

## 3. What fits and what is excluded

The verticals, desks or product lines that qualify, then the exclusions.
Always exclude the competitor **category**, described by who its customers
are. Never list competitor names: a name list is unbounded and the search
walks straight around it.

## 4. The signals, each with a window

One sentence per signal, each naming the event and how old its proving page
may be. Six to ten signals.

> A job advertisement in the last 30 days that names an India based recruiter
> working United States shift hours. A public statement in the last 12 months
> about post-quantum cryptography. An incident report in the last 90 days
> naming certificates or expiry as a cause.

# Two rules the engine depends on

**An institution sentence describes a company. A signal sentence describes an
event.** The company index matches descriptions and holds no events, so an
event-shaped sentence there returns the vendors who sell that event. Measured:
buyer-shaped variations returned 15 of 15 real institutions; event-shaped ones
returned vendors.

**Every signal needs a window.** The engine enforces it as arithmetic and
refuses anything older. A signal with no window is a signal with no recency.

# What only a person can tell you

Ask for these four, and nothing a website already answers:

1. Best accounts, named, and how large they grew.
2. Where the money decision sits, and which sizes are cold.
3. Who must never be contacted.
4. What they want to sell next.

Do not ask what they sell, what their verticals are, or for proof assets. The
site answers those better, and asking produces "competitive rate".
