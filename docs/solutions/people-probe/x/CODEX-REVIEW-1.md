1. **M-SEQ is strong, but the comparison is not controlled.** Exhaustive, effectively free Clay rosters make it a near-oracle retrieval baseline. M-GATE is handicapped by paid calls and the prohibition on overlapping provider slices, while M-SEQ is handicapped if its selector lacks M-GATE’s explicit persona plan. Hold constant the rubric, selector model, candidate-ID protocol, candidate/context limits, verification budget, and top-pick count. Vary only retrieval policy. Book model-token costs too; otherwise “same set at lower cost” structurally favors free Clay.

2. **Union recall is endogenous.** A method’s unique discoveries enlarge the denominator and lower competitors’ recall; shared blind spots disappear entirely. Correlated methods can therefore appear high-recall while missing the same buyer class. Cheapest independent check: create a blinded reference set from Clay’s full seniority-partitioned roster before examining method outputs. Independently label every plausible senior candidate against the locked rubric, then spend Exa plus open-web verification only on positives. Score methods against those candidate IDs. This is Clay-bounded recall, not absolute recall, but it is non-circular and nearly free.

3. **The judge gate is badly underpowered.** Even 0 false positives among 20 negatives has a one-sided 95% upper FPR bound of about 14%, not 5%. Worse, stale and wrong-employer examples test verification more than buyer fit, leaving only 10 function controls—about a 26% upper bound at 0/10. To support `<5%` after observing zero errors requires at least 59 held-out negatives, never reused during judge repair. Add positives so an always-negative judge cannot pass. The missing control is a current, senior, correct-employer, plausible decision-maker whose function matches ICP vocabulary but contradicts client intent: Ondato compliance/AML/KYC leaders versus product/growth onboarding owners.

4. **M-LOOP can certify a wrong ontology as complete.** If the planner omits growth and invents compliance personas, its coverage checker sees every planned persona covered and stops. It can also create artificial “gaps” by renaming personas. Replace prose coverage with observed IDs: against the independent Clay reference set, report rubric-positive candidates/functions present in the roster but absent from round-one retrieval and selection. Round-two marginal value is recovered reference IDs—not whether the planner says its own plan is covered.

5. **The decision thresholds are noise at this sample.** Four GATE-only wins and zero SEQ-only wins gives an exact two-sided paired sign-test `p=0.125`; it does not separate methods. At 20 companies, the minimum clean separation is **6–0 discordant wins** (`p=0.031`). Losses must count through McNemar/sign testing; “four wins” cannot ignore ten losses. Detecting a true 20-point company-level lift with roughly 80% power needs about **39 paired companies** even under the optimistic assumption of no reverse wins. A two-point precision allowance is especially indefensible: merely estimating 90% precision within ±2 points needs about **865 independent people**; a powered between-method comparison needs thousands and company clustering increases that.

6. **Highest-value cheap experiment:** on five Ondato accounts, run a blinded selector ablation over identical full Clay rosters: raw ICP versus the explicit product/growth rubric, same model, IDs, and budget; have the client label the top three. This isolates the dominant failure before paying for retrieval.

**Do not run BrightData enrichment.** It happens after the experimental outcome, cannot improve buyer discovery, and adds a known hanging path plus irrelevant cost.
