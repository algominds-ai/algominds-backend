1. Goal and rules

Take the GTM engine to 10/10: the right companies for any ICP and the right decision makers at them, verified, no per-company cap, at scale, at reasonable cost and speed. Rules that held: five companies per account per run; the engine changes only when an eval proves it; no hardcoded passes; Braintrust for every score; codex (gpt-6-astra, high effort, one resumed session) as adversarial advisor on every critical decision; Sonnet sub-agents for mechanical work.

2. The ruler

eval/run.ts --arm <arm> --trials 1 --count 5 [--profile <slug>] --port <port> runs a companies run then a people run per profile into a local database eval_<arm> and logs one Braintrust experiment per arm. Keys live on branch p2/base: eval/keys/<slug>.json and eval/keys/people/<slug>.json. eval/rescore.ts --arm <arm> replays a finished arm against the current keys and scorer.

Seven hard profiles: mstone, aris, form3, carta, ondato, dental, hvac. Composite: company_yield A/5, buyer_precision T/P, buyer_coverage B/5, engine_quality = coverage × precision, engine_score = gates × quality, rating = 10 × mean. Gates: every delivered row labelled, no key-rejected row delivered, no duplicate organisation, proving holds, both runs complete, per-profile time and cost ceilings. One wrong or unlabelled delivered row zeroes the profile. Contested rulings were adjudicated blind by codex (exports/codex-brutal.md).

Diagnosed, fix on a branch: division and subsidiary records judged as companies (Sainsbury's, Decathlon Digital, Lloyds Bank); proof from a personal profile (Mollie); a mixed-channel company accepted on its consumer line (SteadyPay, three times); sub-function marketing heads delivered as buyers (Midnite); empty companies from empty rosters (Badoo, Taboo); an unknown verification verdict for a real executive (Kontigo); a defunct company proven from historical copy (Monolith).

9. Environment notes

  • One eval per worktree at a time: two dev servers collide on port 9229 and the wrangler state directory.
  • One gate at a time on the machine: concurrent gates share algo_test and the secrets store and produce false failures.
  • Wait loops: pgrep -f '^bun .*scripts/gate\.mjs', never a pattern that matches its own shell.
  • Key commits use --no-verify after bunx biome check eval/keys; never serialize keys with ASCII escaping.
  • Each eval worktree needs .env and .wrangler/state/v3/secrets-store copied from the root; the store now holds gl-api-key.
  • Identical judge inputs on one commit hit the AI Gateway cache at $0; change the input to force a fresh call.
  • Spend watchdog: launcher scripts sum run.cost_dollars over eval_p2* every 30 s and kill past a cap.

10. How to continue

Zero cost first: the frozen regression set eval/regression/judge-rows.json on p2/base (every judged company row with its key ruling) and bun eval/judge-replay.ts --regression score a judge change for cents on precision and retention per profile. The selector replay does the same for the Midnite roster.

Then one repair at a time: regression set, then one profile live ($1.5 to $3.5), then a seven-profile draw ($8) only when Form3 and Ondato both pass their screen bar: five accepted distinct companies, every delivered person accepted, buyers at four of five, within ceilings. Three seven-profile draws, about $24, are the minimum for a rating with a band.

The two repairs with the most evidence: entity and evidence alignment (proof operator equals judged company; size bars applied to the resolved entity) and buyer coverage (roster keywords from the rubric's named titles; a corroborated second source when a verification verdict is unknown).

What 10 requires: every profile fulfils five distinct accepted companies and its buyer requirements within frozen gates, repeatedly. No price buys a guarantee.
